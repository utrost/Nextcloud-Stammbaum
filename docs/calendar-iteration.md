# Alpha.25: reviewed Nextcloud Calendar events

Stammbaum now offers **Add to Nextcloud Calendar** from an authorized person. The researcher selects one writable VEVENT calendar, reviews a title, optional user-entered location, date and audience, then creates a single event. One-time appointments may be all-day or start at an explicit time in an IANA timezone; timed appointments last one hour. Exact full birth and death dates can supply a yearly all-day anniversary. Other historical date forms cannot silently become an anniversary, but the user can enter a separate appointment date. A 29 February anniversary repeats only in leap years. Dates are selected against the account's browser timezone and reviewed before publication. The event includes only the reviewed fields and an access-checked route back to the person; it has no attendees, inferred age, automatic biography or confidential notes.

The Calendar app owns the event and its independent sharing. A living person, unknown living status or management-only person is excluded unless the current tree role and export grant permit explicit inclusion. A linked Contacts card with a birthday causes a second, server-enforced acknowledgement before an additional birthday event is created. This signal does not prove the dates match, and unrelated or inaccessible cards are not scanned. The selected calendar's sharing state is not yet classified; the review explicitly warns that readers and synchronized devices may retain copied details. The built-in generated Contacts birthday calendar is excluded from writable choices.

The adapter uses Nextcloud 34's public `OCP\Calendar\IManager` discovery and `ICreateFromString::createFromStringMinimal` for foreground server-side VEVENT creation. Calendar 6.6.5 is pinned in the disposable CI fixture. A five-minute review binds person, membership and tree revisions, options and destination identity. Rechecks occur immediately before and after the external write. A random event UID and object name plus a private operation receipt support retries without making a duplicate event. Uncertain outcomes are reported as such. If authority changes during publication, the external copy may already exist; Stammbaum suppresses the active link and explains that cleanup in Calendar may be needed. The two apps do not offer an atomic cross-app transaction.

Links belong to the acting account. Refresh distinguishes a missing or moved event from a linked one, and **Unlink event** removes only the Stammbaum connection; it leaves the Calendar event intact. Account deletion/recreation removes links, while credential invalidation prevents pending publication. Pure Calendar operations do not change genealogy revisions. The core app remains usable when Calendar is disabled.

This is a delivered person-context increment toward [#81](https://github.com/utrost/Nextcloud-Stammbaum/issues/81), [#82](https://github.com/utrost/Nextcloud-Stammbaum/issues/82) and [#84](https://github.com/utrost/Nextcloud-Stammbaum/issues/84), not closure of their full criteria. Shared-event entry points, collection audience classification, event ETag conflict/edit/delete review, personal Connections view, stronger cross-app fault injection and full [#86](https://github.com/utrost/Nextcloud-Stammbaum/issues/86) acceptance remain open. The original release-0.15 parity scope is separate.

Verification uses fictional people and calendars. Focused browser tests exercise leap-day recurrence, timed appointments, missing destinations, unlink versus delete, idempotent retries, linked-contact birthday acknowledgement, missing export grants, hidden people, revoke/regrant, stale review and daylight-saving gaps/overlaps. Screenshots at 1440, 390 and 320 pixels are inspected, and optional Calendar absence is checked in the disposable fixture. CI and Alice evidence should be recorded with the release; documentation alone is not acceptance evidence.
