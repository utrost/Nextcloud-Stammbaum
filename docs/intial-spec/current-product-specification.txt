            Stammbaum — current product
            specification
            An illustrated, nontechnical product handoff
            Document version 1.0 · 6 November 2026 · Application reference: release 0.15

            Stammbaum is a private workspace for maintaining family history together. It combines
            people and relationships with the evidence, uncertainty, original records and working notes
            behind them. A family tree is both a collection of information and a boundary for deciding
            who may see or change it.

            This document describes the functionality available in the current application. It is intended
            for a product owner, designer or delivery team that needs to understand or recreate its
            behavior. It does not select a technology, prescribe an architecture or propose a Nextcloud
            redesign.

            How to read this specification. Chapters 1–12 describe current behavior and its limits.
            Examples describe expected outcomes; they are not new test results. Appendix A provides
            acceptance scenarios, Appendix B preserves links to requirement identifiers, and Appendix
            C identifies the evidence used. The broader approved specification includes work that is
            still unfinished: inclusion there does not establish that a feature is available today.

            All people, accounts, documents and media in the examples and screenshots are fictional.
            The screenshots are from the running release 0.15 application, captured on 31 October
            2026. Some show the German interface; their captions explain the relevant behavior in
            English. They illustrate functionality rather than prescribe a pixel-for-pixel design. Bright
            magenta areas deliberately conceal test passwords and credentials.

            Purpose, scope and guiding rules
            Intended users and outcomes
            Stammbaum serves a small, closed group researching and maintaining family history. A
            reader can explore a branch and inspect its evidence. A contributor can add relatives,
            correct a date, attach a document or record an alternative explanation. A tree manager
            controls membership, confidential information and reviewed imports. An installation
            operator maintains access and recovery for the application.

            The current feature set supports these end-to-end activities:

                  Create a private tree, invite collaborators and define their rights.
                  Build a family from individual people and explicitly described connections.
                  Explore ancestors, descendants and the surrounding family without duplicating
                  records.
                  Record sources, quotations, alternative names and dates, and judgments about
                  evidence.
                  Maintain repeated or shared life events, biographies, notes and structured details.
                  Preserve photographs and documents, link them to records and choose portraits.
                  Review a supported genealogy exchange file before importing it into an empty tree.
                  Export the current permitted information, with explicit warnings about what the
                  exchange format cannot preserve.
                  Review changes, resolve concurrent edits, remove people from active use and restore
                  them with controlled consequences.
                  Give an external tool limited access and send minimal notifications about changes.

            An account with a commercial genealogy service is not needed for ordinary use. Existing
            information can be entered manually or transferred through the supported file-import
            workflow. The application does not automatically search commercial records or discover
            relatives.


            Rules that apply throughout
            Unknown information remains unknown. A missing parent, incomplete date or unknown
            living status does not authorize the application to invent a value. A relationship label is
            explicit information, not a conclusion drawn from a name or sex.

            Presentation and historical judgment remain separate. The name or date chosen for a
            card can still be disputed. A neatly displayed tree is not a claim that all its information is
            certain.

            Original material and current research have distinct roles. An imported original is
            retained unchanged. Corrections affect the current research record while preserving its
            connection to the original. A fresh export describes current research within the export
            format's limits.

            Access applies to the whole operation. Permission to see a person does not
            necessarily permit reading a shared citation or changing a shared event. Every affected
            record matters.

            Actions have specific consequences. Removing a connection, deleting a person,
            detaching a photograph and deleting its media record are different actions. The interface
            must communicate which action is being taken.

            Changes must not silently overwrite one another. When the information changed after
            a form was opened, the user's draft is retained for comparison and deliberate
            reconciliation.


            Product boundaries
            A user account identifies a collaborator. A person record describes someone in family
            history. Creating a person does not create an account, send an invitation or grant access.
            Several people may have the same name. The same real individual can be recorded
            independently in two trees; those records do not synchronize.

            The application currently offers a substantial working subset of its approved long-term
            scope. The limitations in Chapter 12 are part of this handoff, not suggestions to silently
            remove unfinished obligations from the original specification.

            People, accounts and permissions
            Accounts and entry to the application
            The first account is created through a one-time installation setup process. There is no
            permanent default password. Subsequent collaborators join through invitations; there is no
            open public registration journey.

            A user can sign in, sign out, change their password and save their interface-language
            preference. A password change requires the current password and signs the user out. An
            operator can issue a single-use recovery link when a password is lost. A recovery link lasts
            30 minutes and does not grant new tree memberships. Application restarts require browser
            users to sign in again.

            Repeated sign-in, invitation, password and recovery attempts can be temporarily refused
            when protective limits are reached. These limits use 15-minute windows. The refusal does
            not delete an account or its research, and retrying repeatedly is not a way to bypass it.

            Operators can inspect account status, enable or disable accounts and review
            account/security activity. Disabling an account preserves its contributed information and
            attribution. The application protects the last active operator and the last active owner of a
            tree against changes that would leave those responsibilities unassigned. Full account
            provisioning and administration beyond the implemented controls remain unfinished.


            Four roles within each tree
            A user may be an owner in one tree and a viewer in another. Roles are not inherited
            across trees. A new tree's creator becomes its owner.

            “Yes” always remains subject to record visibility and shared-record checks. For example,
            an editor cannot edit a source whose existing citations include inaccessible information. A
            manager cannot alter another manager's grant or their own manager grant. An owner can
            manage all role levels but cannot remove the final active owner.

            Any signed-in active account can create a new tree. That ability is separate from the role
            held in an existing tree. The installation operator has no automatic right to view or edit a
            tree through the application. The operator's responsibility for the installation and its
            backups should not be confused with a promise that the host operator is technically unable
            to access stored information.


            Visibility and living status
            Content is either available to tree members or restricted to management only. New
            ordinary person records default to tree-member visibility. Contacts and religious-affiliation
            details are confidential. Managers and owners control visibility changes. A newly uploaded
            medium starts management-only for a manager/owner and member-visible for an editor.

            Living status has three values: living, deceased and unknown. It is distinct from visibility.
            Deceased people are not automatically public. Living and unknown-status people can be
            visible to authorized members inside a closed tree, but both categories are omitted from
            exports by default.

            A restricted person is excluded from unauthorized search results and diagrams. The
            application does not reveal them through a named placeholder or use them as a hidden
            connection between otherwise visible people. Home-person settings cannot expose an
            inaccessible person's identity.

            Shared citations and media require access to every relevant linked target. If a citation is
            attached to a visible person and an inaccessible person, the reader cannot see the citation
            merely because one attachment is visible. Historical private material remains protected
            even if the current person becomes visible to more members.


            Export rights
            Viewers and editors need an additional export grant. Managers and owners have export
            rights through their roles. Downgrading a manager or owner starts with the separate export
            permission unchecked; the person changing the grant must deliberately decide whether to
            retain export access.

            Export permission permits only the information the user is currently allowed to see. It does
            not turn a viewer into a manager or reveal confidential records. It governs Stammbaum's
            export functions; it cannot prevent someone from copying information already displayed to
            them.

            Acceptance example. An editor can correct a visible birthplace and attach an ordinary
            photograph. The same editor cannot change that person's visibility, inspect a
            management-only contact record or delete the person. Giving the editor export permission
            adds export capability without adding those management rights.

            Family trees and navigation
            Creating and managing a tree
            The family-tree list contains the trees accessible to the signed-in user, with their name,
            description, own role and available actions. A tree needs a name; its description is
            optional. Equal names are allowed and the interface supplies a short distinguishing
            identifier. Creating a tree creates an empty private workspace, not a sample family.

            Owners and managers can edit tree details. A tree's management page links to its people,
            evidence library, interactive workspace, import, media and export areas. Management-only
            controls include members, invitations, access activity, deleted people and notifications.
            Available controls follow the user's actual permissions.

            An empty tree supports creating its first person or, for management, reviewing a GEDCOM
            import. There is no implemented merge-into-existing-tree journey or full-tree deletion
            workflow.


            Searching and opening people
            People can be searched by their authorized names, including alternative names, and
            display notes. The current search finds matching text without case sensitivity. It does not
            promise spelling correction, fuzzy matching or equivalence between forms such as “ä” and
            “ae.” Results are loaded in pages; the first loaded page is not the whole tree.

            A search result can be selected even when it lies outside the displayed branch. The details
            panel indicates that situation. The user can then explicitly make the person the focus of a
            new branch. Finding a record does not create another copy of it.


            Focus, selection, home and camera
            Four concepts remain distinct:


              Concept                 User meaning                                   What changing it affects

              Selection               The person currently being inspected           The details panel and available actions

              Camera/view             Diagram mode, generations, zoom and position   How the branch is presented


            Selecting a card or search result opens details without changing the branch or camera.
            Show family branch changes the focus and centers that person while retaining zoom.
            Previous branch returns to preceding branch states, including their focus, selection,
            generations and camera. Up to 30 preceding branch entries are retained.

            The user can choose family, ancestor or descendant view. Upward and downward
            generation depths are separate, from zero through eight; their initial values are three and
            two. Family view includes visible partners and focus siblings where the supported
            relationships or family contexts establish them.

            The diagram shows each person once. Additional paths point to the same person;
            repeated ancestors do not become duplicate records. Different parent relationships and
            partnerships use distinguishable line styles and a text legend. Connections that do not fit
            ordinary generation rows have an explanatory reference style.


            Moving around the workspace
            Users can drag the diagram background, use zoom buttons, fit the view, zoom around the
            pointer with the mouse wheel and use supported two-finger gestures. Keyboard arrows
            move the camera; plus/minus zoom and Home fits the diagram when the diagram has
            focus. These actions do not resize the details panel or change genealogy.

            A text view provides the displayed people and relationship types as navigable text and
            buttons. It is an alternative to the diagram, not another dataset. A selected boundary
            person can be expanded locally, or made the focus to explore a different branch.

            The current view is bounded at 200 unique people and 1,000 relationship lines. A limited-
            view message explains when the full requested neighborhood cannot be displayed. There
            are also limits on local expansions and the number of neighboring records inspected; see
            Chapter 12. A limit is not evidence that excluded relatives do not exist.


            Personal and shared starting points
            Each account has separate saved views and a personal home person for each tree.
            Viewers may change their own navigation preferences without gaining editing rights.

            Managers and owners can set or clear the tree's shared home person. On opening a tree,
            an accessible saved focus takes precedence, followed by personal home, shared home
            and the first visible person. The Home person action uses personal home first and
            otherwise shared home. Clearing personal home lets the user follow the shared default
            without erasing their saved camera or branch history.

            Changing the shared home does not overwrite other people's preferences. If the stored
            person is no longer accessible, the interface uses an authorized alternative or reports
            unavailability without revealing hidden details. A copied view link requires authorized sign-
            in; it is not a public share link.

            Saving an edit or a newly connected relative from the workspace returns to the prior focus
            and camera, with the saved person selected. A failed diagram load exposes a reload
            action instead of presenting an old drawing as a successful fresh result.

            Acceptance example. Ada is the focus. Selecting Clara shows Clara's details while Ada
            remains the focus. Choosing Clara's family branch changes the branch; Previous branch
            returns to Ada's former view. Neither action changes the family records.

            People, dates and relationships
            The person record
            Editors, managers and owners can create and edit people they are allowed to access. The
            basic editor records given name, family name, a display note, living status, sex, visibility,
            birth and death dates/places, and a note explaining the change's provenance. Sex offers
            female, male, unknown and another recorded indication; it does not determine a parent or
            partner role.

            Unknown living status and sex are valid starting values. A person can have an incomplete
            name. Creating someone with neither given nor family name requires explicit confirmation;
            the application does not fabricate a surname. An empty birth or death section does not
            create an invented event.

            Person details bring together life dates, events and notes, claims and evidence, family
            contexts, current connections, assigned media and available history. The preferred
            biography and preferred portraits are presentations of linked records, not replacements for
            those records.

            Correcting a preferred name, date or place retains the earlier assertion and its evidence
            links. An unchanged value does not create an artificial alternative. Clearing a date or place
            makes the current preference empty while retaining the previous information for research
            and history.


            Historical dates and places
            Dates support the following distinctions:


              Date form                 Meaning and example

              Exact or partial          A known day, month or year: “1890-03-12,” “1890-03” or “1890”

              About                     Approximate date, such as “about 1890”

              Before / after            A boundary without a claimed exact date

              Between                   An event occurred somewhere between two dates

              From / to                 A period, including an open start or end where supported

              Calculated                A date derived by calculation, kept distinct from direct evidence

              Estimated                 A research estimate

              Literal wording           Text whose meaning should remain uninterpreted, such as “03/04”

            Structured date entry uses year, year-month or year-month-day. Impossible calendar dates
            are rejected. Partial dates remain partial: a year-only date does not acquire January 1.
            Historical wording remains visible. Ambiguous dates, dates before the common era and
            other calendars can be retained as literal wording; there is no automatic historical-calendar
            conversion.

            Birth/death contradictions produce explanatory findings without silently changing either
            assertion. For example, living status can conflict with a recorded death, or a precise death
            date can precede a precise birth. These findings are not a complete research-quality case-
            management system.

            Places retain entered or imported wording. A place and an address are distinct information.
            No automatic map lookup, geographic normalization or geocoding is provided.


            Adding and connecting relatives
            From a person or the workspace, choose a new relative or search for an existing person.
            Specify whether the connection is parent, child or partner. Parent relationships explicitly
            distinguish unknown, biological, adoptive, foster, step and other.

            Creating a new relative and its connection is one operation: either both become available
            or neither is created. Connecting an existing person reuses that person. Multiple parents
            and partners are supported. An additional partnership episode needs explicit confirmation.

            Names, sex and partnership do not imply parenthood. Two people being parents in the
            same grouping does not itself establish that they were partners. Recording a shared
            marriage event and creating a partnership connection are distinct operations.

            The application refuses self-connections, duplicate active exact parent connections,
            connections across different trees and biological ancestry loops that would make someone
            their own ancestor. Repeated ancestors through different legitimate branches are
            permitted. Nonbiological loops are handled as references in the diagram; a broader social-
            loop warning system is unfinished.

            Removing a connection keeps both people. It does not record a divorce, separation or
            death. The people and their other connections remain available, and evidence/history is
            retained subject to its visibility rules.


            Family contexts and siblings
            A family context groups existing people with parent/child roles, optional role descriptions, a
            label, visibility and explicit child order. It can reference existing parent or partnership
            connections. A person can participate in more than one context, but appears only once
            within a particular context.

            Contexts support siblings whose parents are unknown: create the people, then group them
            in child roles. No placeholder parent is invented. Grouping never creates parent
            relationships or a partnership automatically. Half-siblings and different parental
            circumstances can be represented through the actual connections and separate contexts.

            Child order is a recorded research choice. Displaying a family does not silently rewrite it by
            date. An inactive relationship can remain visible as historical information in a context;
            editing that context offers active relationship choices and drops inactive context references
            on saving, without deleting the underlying history.

            There is no dedicated comprehensive sibling-creation wizard or automatic family
            reconstruction. Users work with the existing relative editor and context controls.

            Acceptance example. Two fictional siblings have no known parents. An editor creates
            their records, places them in a family context as children and orders them. The resulting
            context contains those children and no invented parent or partnership.

            Evidence, alternatives and uncertainty
            Sources and citations
            A source is a reusable description of underlying material: a register, certificate, book,
            interview or other research source. Its fields include title, type, author, publication
            information, repository, call number, external address, description and visibility. Users can
            search source titles, create/edit sources and browse their citations.

            A citation identifies a particular passage or reference within a source. It can record a page
            or other locator, literal quotation/transcription, external address, document date, separate
            access date, reliability and reasoning. Reliability can be unassessed, low, medium or high;
            it is not automatically a verdict on every claim using the citation.

            A citation can refer to a person generally or target particular names, birth/death date or
            place assertions, connections and supported statements. Each specific target can be a
            general reference, support or contradiction. Person-level references are general
            references, not evidence that every statement about that person is true.

            One citation can serve several claims or people. Reusing it preserves one shared citation.
            Detaching one target retains the source, citation and its other targets. Unattached citations
            remain discoverable through their source. Editing a citation from one person's page must
            retain its targets elsewhere, including those outside the currently loaded page.

            An external source address is a link, not a locally preserved document. The application
            does not fetch it automatically. To preserve a supported document locally, upload it through
            the media library and explicitly assign it.


            Alternative assertions and display preferences
            People can have alternative birth names, married names, aliases and other names, and
            alternative birth/death dates. An assertion has its own assessment: unassessed,
            supported, disputed or rejected.

            Choosing a preferred display value and assessing its historical credibility are separate
            decisions. A disputed or rejected assertion can remain the display preference. Adding a
            citation does not automatically change an assessment. A correction does not erase older
            cited values.

            Example: a register suggests a birth in 1890 while a later document says 1891. Both can
            be retained with their own evidence. The researcher can display 1890 and mark it
            disputed, with an explanation. The application does not vote between sources or invent a
            certainty score for the entire person.

            The current alternative-assertion editor does not extend to every possible competing event
            or relationship hypothesis. Multiple events and multiple relationships can exist, but a
            complete system for grouping mutually exclusive event/relationship explanations is
            unfinished.

            Shared evidence and confidentiality
            The reader must be entitled to the citation's source and all its targets. If one dependency is
            inaccessible, the citation is withheld as a whole, including its quotation and target list. An
            editor cannot change a source when doing so would affect inaccessible citing material.

            Changing current visibility does not retroactively expose private historical assertions. A
            private alternative cannot simply become the preferred assertion visible to all tree
            members; an explicitly released assertion is needed. Authors also need to choose
            appropriate visibility for the wording of independently authored source or context labels.

            No general deletion/purge interface for sources, citations, alternatives or family contexts is
            currently supplied. Editing, target detachment and context membership changes have
            narrower consequences and must not be described as permanent deletion.

            Acceptance example. A citation supports one recorded date and contradicts another.
            Marking the first date preferred changes the card's display but not either citation effect.
            Removing the second target leaves the citation linked to the first date.

            Events, biographies and personal details
            Repeated and shared events
            Editors and above can add or edit supported events and details for accessible people.
            Available event choices include marriage, civil marriage, separation/divorce, adoption,
            military service, occupation, education, residence, migration, baptism/burial and explicitly
            labelled custom events. The broader imported-event vocabulary also includes records
            such as census, probate, wills and other supported religious or life events.

            An event/detail records its category and type, title or custom designation, description, date,
            place, visibility, structured fields, participants and provenance. Birth/death card values and
            their alternatives continue to be maintained through the person/evidence editor. Imported
            birth/death statements remain identifiable; they are not silently replaced by an unrelated
            event.

            Events can repeat. One shared event can have multiple participants, each with a role,
            including a custom role. It appears in each participant's timeline as the same record.
            Saving asks the user to confirm the affected people. Editing requires access to all affected
            participants and shared evidence; the original person or family-context association cannot
            silently disappear. Additional participants may be removed explicitly.

            A current/ongoing flag is supported and cannot be combined with an explicit end date.
            Dates can remain partial, approximate or open-ended. The application does not infer
            missing start or end dates.


            Timeline behavior
            The timeline shows life dates first, then loaded additional records by date, with undated
            entries after dated entries. Further pages must be loaded when offered. It is a view of
            loaded records, not an unlimited, globally sorted chronology of the entire tree.

            Entries expose available dates, places, text, participants and a route to their
            sources/citations. Age is shown only when the necessary exact full dates are compatible.
            Approximate years do not produce a falsely precise age.

            The optional relatives' life-events view adds marked context from loaded visible
            relationships. These entries remain relatives' events; showing them does not save extra
            events on the selected person.


            Notes and biographies
            Notes have a title, text and visibility. Basic formatting supports paragraphs, emphasis, bold
            text, lists and deliberate links. HTML and embedded-image instructions are displayed
            literally rather than executed or loaded. Opening a note does not fetch external resources.

            A person can have several notes. Selecting one as the preferred biography controls the
            main narrative presentation; other notes remain separate. Imported notes retain their
            original provenance when corrected.


            Contacts, attributes and structured details
            Contacts and attributes can repeat. Structured fields preserve values, their labels, units
            and ordering, including name components, address parts, organization or qualification
            information where supported. These are not collapsed into a single undifferentiated text
            field.

            Contacts and religious affiliation remain management-only. A genealogy email address
            does not create an account, issue an invitation or send a message. Leading zeros and
            literal wording in contact information are retained. Imported contact/address information
            may conservatively restrict its containing person or family record to management; the
            import review explains that treatment.

            An imported statement's primary wording and recognized detail fields can be corrected
            separately. The original file, original structure, provenance and citation targets remain
            retained. Imported type/category/subject are not freely reassigned. Values outside the
            supported editing limits remain readable but may be read-only. Changing a name-
            components detail does not silently change the separately preferred display name.


            Editing and confirmation
            Switching interface language preserves the open draft. Cancelling or leaving an open
            statement prompts before discarding unsaved entries. A concurrent change retains the
            draft and requires loading current information, comparison and explicit acknowledgement
            before retrying. Saving a shared event is its own completed action; cancelling a different
            form does not undo it.

            Acceptance example. A researcher records one fictional marriage with two participants.
            Both timelines show that event. Correcting its place updates the shared record for both. No
            duplicate marriage is created, and the old import wording remains available as provenance
            where applicable.

            Photographs, documents and other media
            Uploads and original files
            The media library stores private JPEG/PNG originals and PDF attachments. A file may be
            at most 10 MiB; images also have dimension and pixel-count limits. PDFs are
            downloadable attachments, not an inline document viewer. Other formats require
            conversion outside the application.

            Editors and above can upload media and maintain metadata they are authorized to
            change. Metadata includes title, description, visibility, capture date and digitization date.
            Those dates are separate from upload time and can retain historical uncertainty.

            Image previews do not replace originals. Downloading an original retrieves its unchanged
            stored bytes. Previews omit original embedded metadata. The application does not
            promise automatic interpretation of camera orientation or source-system portrait/crop
            settings.


            Assignments, portraits and crops
            One medium can be assigned to multiple people, events, details, sources, citations or
            family contexts. Each assignment has a meaning. A person assignment can be designated
            a preferred portrait and can carry a reversible crop for presentation.

            Cropping changes the preview presentation, not the original file. Clearing the crop restores
            the full-image presentation. The portrait is currently shown on the person page; tree
            diagram cards retain their existing neutral portrait symbol.

            Reading a shared medium requires visibility of the medium and every linked
            target/dependency. Linking it to a confidential record may therefore prevent an ordinary
            member from viewing it through another visible assignment. Editors cannot use an
            accessible assignment to bypass that restriction.


            Missing imported media
            An imported filename or external address is a reference, not proof that a file has been
            obtained. Missing and external references remain distinguishable from verified local files.
            The application never automatically downloads media from those addresses.

            Managers and owners can upload a bounded ZIP package, review its candidates and
            explicitly match a candidate's full path to a missing or external reference. Equal filenames
            in different folders do not produce an automatic match. Successful matching preserves the
            original reference while attaching verified stored bytes.

            The package limit is 32 MiB compressed, 100 entries and 128 MiB expanded, with a 10
            MiB limit per file. Invalid or unsafe package contents are rejected as a whole rather than
            partly imported. No instruction file inside a package is executed.

            Detaching and deleting
            Detaching a medium from a person removes that association only. It does not delete its

            portrait presentation for one person does not alter the original download or the other

            Import eligibility and supported input
            Only a manager or owner can upload, review, confirm or cancel an import and retrieve its
            original. The destination must be empty of genealogy records, including standalone
            sources. A tree with members or navigation settings is not necessarily populated, but a
            tree already containing research cannot receive an additional import.

            The current bounded profile accepts GEDCOM 5.5/5.5.1 with UTF-8, ASCII or explicitly
            identified UTF-16 text. It does not accept ANSEL, legacy Windows encodings, GEDCOM 7
            or a ZIP as the genealogy input. The maximum GEDCOM size is 8 MiB and the maximum
            imported population is 2,000 people.

            Supported content includes names and name components, life dates and places, explicit
            family connections and ordered contexts, notes, sources/citations, selected additional
            events/attributes, contact details and media references. Preserved extension text is not
            necessarily interpreted as an editable feature. A narrow adoption-event combination is
            supported; arbitrary adoption encodings are not.

            can be imported completely. Unsupported standard information blocks activation.
            Unsupported vendor material can remain preserved with warnings, without being promoted
            to historical claims. The exact import profile and reported limitations are part of the user's
            review.

            Export review and creation
            Users with export rights open GEDCOM export and choose options. By default, living
            people, people with unknown living status and management-only information are omitted.
            Authorized users can deliberately include living/unknown people; management-only
            inclusion additionally requires a manager or owner. An optional ordinary media ZIP can
            include authorized local originals.

            The preview shows included counts and the supported format's limitations. Counts do not
            reveal exact hidden populations. Shared records depending on excluded people or targets
            are excluded too. Export choices do not change the tree's stored visibility or living status.

            after navigating away. When it completes, the requesting user can download the permitted
            result. Results expire after 24 hours. A queued job can be cancelled before publication;
            failed work requires a fresh review and export rather than an unrestricted automatic retry.

            Any tree edit or relevant access change invalidates an older result, even when the change
            would not obviously affect the file. Regaining permission does not revive it. The user must
            review and generate a fresh export. One user's export job is not a general download
            facility for every tree member.


            What the export preserves and loses
            The output uses GEDCOM 5.5.1 and current preferred values, current edited statements
            and active relationships. It does not append obsolete imported wording to reconstruct
            deleted people or removed connections. Superseded preferred names and rejected
            alternatives are excluded; eligible active alternative names can be included. Choosing an
            older assertion as preferred can make it current again.

            Collaboration, history and recovery
            Invitations and membership changes
            A manager can invite viewers or editors; an owner can invite all role levels. The invitation
            identifies the tree, role, export permission and expiry. The generated link is single-use and
            lasts 48 hours. The user shares it deliberately; the current workflow does not depend on
            automatic email delivery.

            The recipient reviews the invitation and explicitly accepts, creating an account or using an
            existing account as appropriate. Possessing the link is not the same as anonymous access
            to the tree. Invalid, revoked, expired or already consumed links do not grant a fresh
            membership.

            Owners and managers can review members within their role boundaries. Role/export
            changes and removal require confirmation. A stale grant cannot overwrite a newer grant;
            comparison retains the proposed edit. Any member can leave, except that the final active
            owner must first appoint another active owner.

            Changing or removing a membership revokes outstanding invitations issued by that
            member. Fresh invitations are required if still needed. A removed member's genealogy and
            attribution remain. An old invitation or previously opened form does not regain authority
            merely because the person later rejoins.


            Concurrent changes and uncertain saves
            Forms are based on the version the user opened. If another user saves first, the later
            attempt does not overwrite it silently. The application retains the draft, explains the conflict
            and offers current information for comparison. The user reconciles the differences and
            deliberately retries. Some editors, including shared statements, require an explicit
            comparison acknowledgement.

            Loading current information is not an automatic merge. It must not be represented as
            evidence that every difference has been resolved. Personal navigation preferences also
            detect changes from another tab, with explicit reload or overwrite choices after checking
            the current version.

            An interrupted connection may leave the user uncertain whether a save completed.
            Supported create workflows recognize a repeated unchanged request rather than creating
            a duplicate. The interface does not indiscriminately replay every failed write or promise that
            an unsaved draft survives closing the browser. Changes made after access is withdrawn
            must be refused even if the form was already open.

            Genealogy history and access activity
            Person history exposes available revisions and attributed changes, subject to current
            access and historical confidentiality. A provenance note explains an operation; it is not

            source, citation, family context and relationship are unfinished even when underlying
            changes retain attribution.

            timestamps, stable actor/target identifiers and relevant role/export changes. Users can
            filter by action and outcome, refresh and load further entries. It contains no family narrative

            Deleting and restoring people
            Managers and owners review the impact before deleting a person. Deletion removes the
            person from active searches, diagrams and exports, deactivates active relationships and
            clears a matching shared home. Shared sources, statements, events and media remain
            retained rather than being broadly erased.

            The deleted-people area lists the latest 100 deletions and offers restoration. Restoring
            brings back the person, not their former active connections. Those connections must be
            reviewed and explicitly recreated; normal ancestry checks still apply. This avoids
            automatically reviving old errors or inappropriate links.

              Term                          Meaning in this specification

              Token                         A named, limited and expiring credential for an external tool

              Webhook                       A signed notification sent to an explicitly configured receiving service
